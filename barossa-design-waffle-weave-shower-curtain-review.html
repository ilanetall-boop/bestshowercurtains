What We Liked

The strongest case for the Barossa Design waffle weave shower curtain is its hardware. Twelve rust-resistant metal grommets and a reinforced top header are the two details that most often decide whether a budget fabric curtain holds up, and both are present here at a price under $22. Cheaper curtains in this category often cut corners on grommet count or skip the reinforced header entirely, which is usually where tearing starts.

The honeycomb waffle pattern also does real work visually. It gives the curtain a textured, spa-like look that reads as a more expensive cotton-blend fabric without the slower drying time heavier cotton panels need. For a bathroom that wants a hotel feel on a budget, that texture-to-price ratio is the curtain's clearest advantage over a flat, printed polyester panel at a similar cost.

Sizing is straightforward too. At a standard 72 by 72 inches, it fits the vast majority of US shower rods and tub enclosures without needing to size up or double a panel, which keeps the per-curtain cost predictable compared with extra-wide or extra-long options that typically cost several dollars more.

What Could Be Better

The clearest gap is the missing liner. Barossa Design sells this as a standalone fabric curtain, which keeps the price down but means you need a separate waterproof liner already in place or budgeted as an add-on cost. Compare that with the Dynamene Ringless set covered below, which bundles a snap-in liner directly into the price.

The listing also does not publish a star rating or review count at the time of writing, so we can't tell you how it holds up after a year of regular washing the way we could if verified buyer data existed. That's a gap in the available information, not a confirmed flaw in the product, but it does mean you're buying on spec sheet and price alone rather than on a track record.

Finally, the polyester and PEVA blend is lighter than the 256 GSM fabric Dynamene uses in its heavyweight waffle curtain, so expect a bit more light transmission through the panel and a slightly less substantial feel in hand. For most bathrooms that's a reasonable tradeoff for the lower price, but if a dense, opaque fabric matters more to you, the heavier alternative is worth the few extra dollars.

Who Is It For?

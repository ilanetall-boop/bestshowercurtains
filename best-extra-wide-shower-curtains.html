Things to Know Before You Buy

  • 72 inches is the practical ceiling for a single panel. Every curtain in this guide tops out at 72 inches wide, a few inches past the 70 to 71 inch standard, so check your rod length before you buy.
  • Material changes how the width behaves. Microfiber and cotton hold their shape and resist billowing better than lightweight polyester or PEVA at this size.
  • A weighted or reinforced hem matters more on wide panels. The extra fabric surface area catches more airflow, so a plain hem is more likely to blow inward during a shower.
  • Hookless designs save a step but limit hook choice. A few picks here use built-in rings, which speeds up installation but means you cannot swap in decorative hooks later.
  • Pair the width with a matching liner. A standard-width liner will sit short of the edges on a 72 inch curtain and reduce splash protection.

Frequently Asked Questions

What counts as an extra-wide shower curtain?
Most standard shower curtains measure 70 to 71 inches wide. Anything at 72 inches or wider, like the curtains in this guide, gives you extra coverage for a larger stall, a clawfoot tub, or a double-width opening where a standard panel leaves gaps at the edges.
Do extra-wide shower curtains need extra hooks?
Yes. A wider panel needs more support points to hang evenly and resist billowing. Owners of the curtains in this guide report better results with a full 12-hook set spaced evenly across the rod rather than the 8 to 10 hooks that work fine on a standard-width curtain.
Can I use an extra-wide curtain with a standard shower curtain liner?
You can, but a standard 70 inch liner will sit a few inches inside the outer curtain and may not fully seal a wider stall. For the best results, pair a 72 inch or wider curtain with a liner of the same width so both panels line up at the rod.

Things to Know Before You Buy

  • A nordic shower curtain leans on texture, not print. Waffle weave and plain neutral tones carry the look on picks like the Amazon Basics and Gorilla Grip curtains, so you won't find a busy pattern in this guide.
  • Measure your rod before you shop. Six of the seven curtains here measure 72 by 72 inches, the standard US tub and stall size. Our size guide covers wider stalls and clawfoot tubs.
  • Material changes the drape. Polyester and PEVA blends hang flat and wipe clean fast, microfiber falls with more weight, and sheer fabric lets in the most light. Pick based on how much privacy your bathroom layout needs.
  • Pair the curtain with the right hardware. A plain nordic-style curtain still needs a rod and rings that match the finish. Our hanging guide walks through the install.
  • Airflow controls mildew resistance more than the fabric does. Even a PEVA panel needs to hang open after each shower. Our mildew prevention guide covers the habits that matter more than the material.

Frequently Asked Questions

What makes a shower curtain nordic style?

A nordic shower curtain relies on texture and a muted, neutral colorway instead of a printed pattern. Waffle weave, matte white, and soft grey tones do the work that a busy print handles on other curtains, which is why picks like the Amazon Basics Waffle Weave Shower Curtain and the Gorilla Grip Waffle Shower Curtain fit the look.

What size shower curtain fits most nordic-style bathrooms?

Most of the curtains in this guide measure 72 by 72 inches, the standard size for a single tub or stall opening in a US bathroom. Confirm your rod width before you buy, since a curtain that's too narrow will gap at the edges and let water past the tub.

Should I choose fabric, PEVA, or microfiber for a nordic shower curtain?

PEVA and polyester blends, like the Amazon Basics and Gorilla Grip picks, wipe down fast and resist mildew better than woven fabric. Microfiber, like the Seenus curtain, feels softer and drapes with more weight. Sheer fabric, like the Madison Park pick, lets in more light but offers less privacy on its own.

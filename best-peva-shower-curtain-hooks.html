Things to Know Before You Buy

  • PEVA vs vinyl: PEVA is a chlorine-free alternative to PVC vinyl, so it skips the strong chemical smell that vinyl curtains often have straight out of the package.
  • Grommet count matters: Curtains with reinforced metal or plastic grommets spread the weight of shower curtain hooks evenly and resist tearing longer than curtains with flimsier holes.
  • Weight and drape: Heavier PEVA sheets hang straighter and cling to the tub less during a shower, which matters when you are pairing the curtain with lightweight hooks.
  • Most PEVA curtains are wipeable, not washable: Check the listing before you toss one in the laundry, since machine washing can warp thinner PEVA sheets.
  • Liner vs stand-alone: Thinner curtains in this guide double as a liner behind a fabric curtain, while the heavier ones work fine on their own.

Frequently Asked Questions

What size shower curtain hooks work with PEVA curtains?
Standard shower curtain hooks and rings, the kind sized for a typical 72-inch shower rod, work with every curtain in this guide since they all use similarly spaced grommets. If a curtain's grommets feel tight, a slightly larger S-hook style glides through more easily than a narrow ring.
Is PEVA better than vinyl for a shower curtain?
PEVA is generally the better choice for indoor air quality because it is chlorine-free and skips the plasticizers used in PVC vinyl, which is why several curtains in this roundup are labeled non-toxic PEVA. Vinyl curtains can be heavier and resist clinging better, but the chemical smell fresh out of the package is usually stronger.
How often should you replace a PEVA shower curtain?
Most owners in the reviews we compared replace a PEVA curtain every 6 to 12 months, depending on bathroom ventilation and how often the curtain gets wiped down. Grommet tearing around the shower curtain hooks is usually the first sign it needs replacing, which is why curtains with reinforced grommets tend to last longer between replacements.
